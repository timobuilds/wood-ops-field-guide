# Release work that can finish

## Job to be done

**Situation.** An order stops or restarts because required inputs are missing, late or incorrect.

**Deliverable.** An order-level release checklist and an exception log with upstream owners.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Start at the interrupted operation. For one order verify five inputs: usable instructions, equipment and tools, material and components, a defined method, and available people with the skills to execute.

2. Check specification, revision, quantity, sequence, time and point of use. Trace the first failed input to its origin. Ask the expediter what they chased; record the recurring cause separately from today’s workaround.

3. Mark release, hold or explicitly authorized exception for the order. Name the check owner and evidence for release. Compare uninterrupted completions, restarts and complete shipments before and after the trial.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** Assembly is surrounded by parts but keeps stopping for one missing door.

## Source lessons

- Trace incomplete shipments upstream through assembly to the first missing or unusable input. [Getting more done: The power of fit and complete](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=6026882&switch_issue_id=60989)
- At assembly, measure time spent identifying, sorting and fetching parts before considering automation. [Where to look for the next boost in productivity?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5986139&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Confirm that every required input fits and is available where and when the next process needs it. [Basics first: Fit & complete](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5889128&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Verify fit and availability across information, tools, material, method and workforce. [Complete and fit: The starting point for productivity](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5672634&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Use interrupted assembly, full buffers and frustrated workers to find fit-and-completeness failures. [Fit & Complete - Productivity Improvement 101](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5411070&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Use the expediter’s record to identify repeat causes of stoppages and test the suspected bottleneck. [Improve by asking the right questions](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161434&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Work backward from the limiting operation to eliminate recurring losses of fit and availability. [Get a huge productivity boost by working on the basics](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161380&switch_issue_id=60989)
