# Estimate the cost and margin of a job

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A quote or product margin looks implausible, especially across different product families.

**Deliverable.** A reconciled cost sheet for one representative job and a sensitivity range.

Name who needs progress, in what circumstance, and how they will measure it. Use the known baseline or identify the smallest observation that would establish one. Label proposed targets.

## Work through the decision

1. Specify quantity, mix, bill of material, yield, direct hours and the basis of the shop rate. Include indirect materials and paid time without counting them twice.

2. Model direct material, direct labour and overhead separately. Show the allocation base and how a simple average can underprice complex products. Distinguish selling price, contribution and fully allocated margin.

3. Reconcile a completed order with actual material, hours and rework. Test volume, wage, yield and mix assumptions. State what should change in the next quote.

## Return to the user

Fill in the deliverable with the evidence at hand. Mark estimates and unknowns. Name the next action, its proposed owner, the review point and the finding that could change the decision. Cite the relevant column for its teaching and identify your proposed application.

**Example request.** Our painted cabinets sell well but the estimated margin never shows up.

## Source lessons

- Check labour-time assumptions, yield, indirect materials and overhead before trusting an estimate. [How sure are you about your estimating?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5911650&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Update the product costing model and examine which assumptions shift margin across products. [Which product makes you money? Do you know your production cost?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5307439&switch_issue_id=60989)
