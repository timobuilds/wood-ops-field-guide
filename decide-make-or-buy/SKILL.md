# Decide what to make or buy

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A component could be outsourced or brought in-house, affecting cost, service and capacity.

**Deliverable.** A sourcing decision by scenario and a supplier or in-house trial.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Distinguish outsourcing existing work, freeing a constraint, and building a new in-house capability. Define what matters to the customer about the component.

2. Compare landed supplier cost and avoidable internal cost. Identify overhead that remains, freed capacity that can actually be sold, new investment, required skills, quality, lead time and coordination risk.

3. Test the partner on representative orders and exceptions. Define acceptance, replenishment, escalation and a fallback. State the volume or service condition at which the choice changes.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** Should we outsource drawer boxes so the router can finish more kitchens?
