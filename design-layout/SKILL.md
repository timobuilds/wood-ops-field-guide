# Choose a plant layout

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A proposed machine move, expansion or rearrangement needs a flow-based decision.

**Deliverable.** A comparison of the current layout and alternatives, with a phased move decision.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Confirm future mix, volume, manufacturing model, supplier strategy and the business objective. Trace travel and waiting for a representative order before changing the floor plan.

2. Sketch status quo, a modest work-cell change and structural alternatives where appropriate. Mark fixed machines, utilities, access, material paths, buffers and space for future volumes. Ask operators where each option creates extra handling.

3. Compare lead time, throughput, disruption, installation and transition cost. Choose a reversible mock-up or trial when possible. Plan phasing and owner review for major moves.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** We want to move the panel saw to make room. Will that improve flow?
