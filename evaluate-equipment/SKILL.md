# Justify equipment or automation

## Job to be done

**Situation.** An equipment or automation proposal needs evidence of its whole-plant benefit.

**Deliverable.** An investment comparison with a break-even condition and a verification trial.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Compare the status quo, process improvement, outsourcing and realistic equipment alternatives. Verify the proposed machine serves the actual constraint and expected demand.

2. Estimate installed cost including tooling, rigging, utilities, training, startup disruption and maintenance. Separate cash savings, released hours, saleable incremental output and one-time inventory release. Avoid counting the same benefit twice.

3. Test representative jobs at realistic mix and utilization. For cut-and-drill versus nesting, vary standardization, volume, batch size, setup and downstream sorting. Report assumptions, downside and the condition that would reverse the decision.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** Should we buy a second nesting router or improve how we feed assembly?

## Source lessons

- Compare alternatives against the fully installed cost and benefits that can actually be realized. [Equipment justification: A practical guide](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=6059294&switch_issue_id=60989)
- At assembly, measure time spent identifying, sorting and fetching parts before considering automation. [Where to look for the next boost in productivity?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5986139&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Compare capital invested with the realistic alternative and distinguish financial returns from qualitative benefits. [Equipment justification](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5958018&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Compare nesting with cutting and drilling using volume, standardization, yield and sequencing. [Cut and drill versus nesting](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5586507&switch_issue_id=60989)
