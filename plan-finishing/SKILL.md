# Specify a repeatable finishing process

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A new finish, coating or finishing line must satisfy appearance, volume and repeatability requirements.

**Deliverable.** A jointly reviewed finish specification, trial matrix and line acceptance criteria.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Start with the agreed appearance on a real substrate and the expected mix and volume. Document preparation, application, drying or curing, handling and inspection as one process.

2. Bring the material and equipment suppliers together. Test the actual substrate, geometry and finish sequence. Record acceptable variation, defects, timing and capacity at each step.

3. Use representative samples to decide readiness. Assign facility specialists to verify site-specific ventilation, storage, permits and other requirements before installation. Record the unresolved questions and acceptance owner.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** We need a new painted finish. What do we prove before ordering the line?
