# Learn from a plant walk

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A plant tour or operational review must reveal where an order waits and what to investigate.

**Deliverable.** An annotated flow map, an observation log and one hypothesis to test.

Turn the request into a draft objective in the user’s terms: “When [circumstance], help [person] achieve [observable progress] so [customer or business outcome].” Add a baseline, desired result, owner and review point if known. Mark proposed targets as proposals. When no baseline exists, specify the smallest observation needed to establish one. Continue with the evidence available.

## Work through the decision

1. Set the purpose of the visit: learn from a peer, evaluate a supplier, or understand your own order flow. Pick a representative order and ask permission before documenting a host’s process.

2. Walk from shipping toward upstream work. Sketch material and information handoffs, queues, handling, visual controls and the real scheduling signal. Ask operators to explain anomalies. Mark observed facts separately from interpretations.

3. Debrief while details are fresh. Have observers compare maps, identify what may differ at home, and choose one transferable experiment. For process mapping, validate the current path with the people doing the work before drawing a future path.

## Return to the user

Produce the deliverable above using the user's facts. Identify the decisive unknown and how to test it. Finish with the next action, proposed owner, review point and the observation that would justify a different decision. Clearly distinguish observed results, estimates and recommendations. Cite the specific column when explaining its teaching. The JTBD and deliverable are adaptations of Sepp Gmeiner’s writing.

**Example request.** We have a factory tour on Friday. What should we look for?

## Source lessons

- Reconstruct the plant’s flow after the walk and identify one practice worth testing back home. [Plant Tours: A shortcut to operational excellence](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=6097115&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Give a peer or supplier visit a specific question and compare notes promptly afterward. [Maximizing the benefits of plant tours](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=6048482&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Prepare a visit around a decision and use the observations for peer, supplier or internal learning. [Plant tours – A simple tool to learn](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5778755&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Observe scheduling, space, quality and visual controls during a purposeful plant visit. [Plant tours – How to read a plant](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5534295&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Map the actual process with participants before redesigning handoffs and information flow. [The importance of process mapping](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161376&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Compare independent observers’ impressions and verify hypotheses about flow, controls and working conditions. [Plant tours and how to read a plant](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161471&switch_issue_id=60989)
