# Prevent repeat quality losses

## Job to be done

**Situation.** Rejects, repair, field service or inconsistent acceptance consume capacity.

**Deliverable.** A defect definition, ranked loss, corrective action and recurrence check.

Name who needs progress, in what circumstance, and how they will measure it. Use the known baseline or identify the smallest observation that would establish one. Label proposed targets.

## Work through the decision

1. Define acceptance with the customer and operator. Use an agreed boundary sample where visual judgement varies. Trace where the defect was introduced, detected and paid for.

2. Estimate the cost of poor quality: scrap, rework labour, occupied machine time, returns, service and replacements. Deduplicate costs; mark estimates. Rank by impact and frequency.

3. Have affected departments test one cause and corrective change. Verify the defect at source and customer impact over a defined follow-up period. Record who owns the new check.

## Return to the user

Fill in the deliverable with the evidence at hand. Mark estimates and unknowns. Name the next action, its proposed owner, the review point and the finding that could change the decision. Cite the relevant column for its teaching and identify your proposed application.

**Example request.** We disagree about what finish quality is acceptable and keep paying for repairs.

## Source lessons

- Rank the cost of failures and select a quality-system practice that addresses the largest actionable loss. [Making quality a priority](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5977087&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Use agreed acceptance limits, inspection feedback and cross-functional review to prevent recurrence. [Increase customer satisfaction and reduce your cost at the same time - a win-win situation](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161287&switch_issue_id=60989)
