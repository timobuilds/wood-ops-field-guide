# Build a factory scoreboard

## Job to be done

**Situation.** Managers need to tell whether performance is improving and decide how to respond.

**Deliverable.** A three-to-five-measure scoreboard with an action trigger for every measure.

Name who needs progress, in what circumstance, and how they will measure it. Use the known baseline or identify the smallest observation that would establish one. Label proposed targets.

## Work through the decision

1. Start with the customer promise and the question workers need answered. Define eligible orders, period, units, source, original promise date and treatment of unshipped orders. Count acceptable output per labour hour where productivity matters.

2. Pair an outcome with an early signal at the constraint and a check on quality, safety or cost. Keep the original baseline, show missing data, and compare like product mixes.

3. Put the figures where operators can explain them. Use a daily review to assign action and a longer trend to judge change. Retire unused metrics; test whether improving a number could worsen the outcome.

## Return to the user

Fill in the deliverable with the evidence at hand. Mark estimates and unknowns. Name the next action, its proposed owner, the review point and the finding that could change the decision. Cite the relevant column for its teaching and identify your proposed application.

**Example request.** Our dashboard is green but customers still complain.

## Source lessons

- Benchmark using consistent definitions, credible samples, subdivisions, confidentiality and timely reporting. [Do you have facts – or just an opinion?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5878905&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Share a small set of measures with operators and review averages and trends alongside daily results. [How is your production doing?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5788908&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Give workers a visible score and interpret trends in customer and production outcomes. [What you measure gets attention, and what gets attention will improve](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5561674&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Agree on objectives and establish a credible baseline and reporting cycle. [How do you know you are doing a good job?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5269345&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Choose measures around customer needs and the constraint; tailor the review to each level of the plant. [Defining the right metrics for your company](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5161487&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Use three to five worker-owned measures tied to decisions; preserve the original promise date and review trends. Being busy is not the same as being productive or efficient, Woodworking Canada, September/October 2026, pp. 42–43 (supplied print pages)
