# Sustain an improvement on the floor

## Job to be done

**Situation.** A recurring source of waste is known or a previous Lean effort faded.

**Deliverable.** A worker-led trial with protected time, an observable standard and a review date.

Name who needs progress, in what circumstance, and how they will measure it. Use the known baseline or identify the smallest observation that would establish one. Label proposed targets.

## Work through the decision

1. Ask a worker to show one task that is harder than it needs to be. Identify the customer value and observe waiting, motion, transport, defects, excess inventory, overprocessing or overproduction.

2. Choose one change sized to the problem. Use 5S for searching and disorganization; for large batches measure setup work and test a shorter changeover. Protect quality and safe work. Identify the sponsor, practical training and scheduled time.

3. Compare the trial with a comparable baseline. Ask the operator whether the work is easier and inspect customer or flow effects. Adopt and document the revised standard, adjust, or reverse. Set a check that will reveal a slide back to the old method.

## Return to the user

Fill in the deliverable with the evidence at hand. Mark estimates and unknowns. Name the next action, its proposed owner, the review point and the finding that could change the decision. Cite the relevant column for its teaching and identify your proposed application.

**Example request.** We cleaned up for a Lean event, but two weeks later the old routine returned.

- Identify customer-value losses; use workplace organization as a starting point and reduce changeovers to permit smaller batches. [Unlocking the potential of Lean manufacturing: Strategies for success](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=6019609&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Look for efficiency opportunities across people, materials, process and technology together. [In pursuit of efficiency - The power of efficiency = E4](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5994550&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Check three prerequisites for Lean implementation: know-how, leadership and protected time. [Lean – Three key elements](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5864822&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Make daily work easier for operators while preserving safety and quality. [Lean manufacturing: Easier, better, faster, cheaper](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5653921&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Use experience from actual Lean adoption to design a manageable first improvement. [Lean manufacturing: It really works](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5631704&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Investigate stalled Lean efforts through management participation, ownership, training and sustained effort. [Lean manufacturing: How often have you tried?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5568993&switch_issue_id=60989)
- Make strategic priorities actionable through leadership, delegation and room for employees to improve work. [What are you going to change now?](https://www.woodworkingcanada.com/r5/showkiosk.asp?listing_id=5526590&switch_issue_id=60989)
