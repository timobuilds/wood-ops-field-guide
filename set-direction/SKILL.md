# Choose an operating priority

## Job to be done

**Situation.** Several departments want improvements; decide where the business needs progress first.

**Deliverable.** A one-page plan naming one outcome, its owner and the first milestone.

Name who needs progress, in what circumstance, and how they will measure it. Use the known baseline or identify the smallest observation that would establish one. Label proposed targets.

## Work through the decision

1. Describe the customer, market, product mix and time horizon. Work backward from a longer-term ambition through a medium-term milestone to the next operating decision.

2. Compare candidate changes across sales, product, people, organization, technology, information and production. Show where a local gain helps or impedes the business objective. Include the cost of staying as we are.

3. Choose one priority, a baseline, a near-term experiment and who has time and authority to own it. Record the other candidates and what evidence would change the ranking.

## Return to the user

Fill in the deliverable with the evidence at hand. Mark estimates and unknowns. Name the next action, its proposed owner, the review point and the finding that could change the decision. Cite the relevant column for its teaching and identify your proposed application.

**Example request.** Our improvement list keeps growing and nobody knows which project comes first.
